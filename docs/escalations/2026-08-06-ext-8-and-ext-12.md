## EXT-8

**To:** Digital Records
**Copy:** Finance (question 2 is a billing blocker), Product
**Subject:** ID Standard and FinGov v1.5 disagree on identifiers. Five questions, one of them visible to users.

Digital Records,

**The published terms promise users a professional category the platform cannot issue
an identifier for.** The terms of service section 2 and the privacy policy section 1
both name nine professional categories. Three of those nine, plumbers, electricians
and interior designers, have no code in either of the two coded lists. Anyone who
reads the terms and signs up in one of those trades is promised a place the identifier
system has no way to record.

**Nothing has been issued yet, so whatever you rule gets encoded once with nothing to
correct.** The platform does not generate professional identifiers at all today. There
is no method for it. Every conflict below is still latent, which makes this a decision
that is free right now and expensive later, once identifiers are in the world, on
documents and behind QR codes.

Five questions.

### 1. Which professional categories does the platform serve, and which list is authoritative?

There are four lists and no two agree.

| Source | Count | Categories |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ID Standard section 4.0 | 6 | LAW, SUR, VAL, AGT, ARC, ENG |
| Platform registration types | 7 | LAWYER, SURVEYOR, VALUER, ARCHITECT, ENGINEER, BUILDER, QUANTITY_SURVEYOR |
| FinGov v1.5 section 5.1 | 13 | the ID Standard's six, plus QSV, BLD, PLN, PJM, CON, SUP, SSP |
| Terms section 2 and Privacy section 1 | 9 | architects, surveyors, engineers, lawyers, builders, plumbers, electricians, quantity surveyors, interior designers, "and other related professionals" |

The counts are the least interesting part. Three things in how the lists overlap matter
more.

**One. The platform's list crosses the ID Standard rather than nesting inside it.**

- It has **Builder** and **Quantity Surveyor**, which **the ID Standard cannot issue
  identifiers for**. So the platform can already model two professional types it could
  never identify.
- It lacks **Estate Agent**, which **both controlling documents code** (`AGT` in the ID
  Standard section 4.0 and in FinGov section 5.1). So it cannot model one type both
  documents expect it to have, which for a real estate business is the strangest gap of
  the four.

**Two. `AGT` and `VAL` appear in no published document at all.** Estate Agent and Valuer
are coded in both controlling documents and named in neither the terms nor the privacy
policy. Estate Agent is not in the platform's list either. So we are carrying codes for
two categories nothing user-facing mentions, while the user-facing documents promise
three the codes do not cover. **Is Estate Agent a category the platform actually serves?**
If the answer is no, `AGT` is vestigial and the question is whether to retire it. If yes,
it is missing from the product and from both published documents.

**Three. The platform's list looks like it was built from the marketing prose, not from
the ID Standard.** The published nine is exactly the platform's seven, minus Valuer, plus
the three uncoded trades. That is very unlikely to be coincidence, and if it is what
happened, then the ID Standard has never been the source of the platform's category list
and the disagreement is older than this epic.

FinGov is a strict superset of the ID Standard on membership, so those two may be a
versioning question rather than a disagreement. Please confirm which way round that is,
because it changes whether this is one decision or two.

Both published documents end their list with **"and other related professionals"**, so
the prose does not close the set. A coded list has to.

**What we need:** the single authoritative list, a yes or no on Estate Agent, and a
ruling on whether the three uncoded trades in the published terms are in scope or should
come out of the published documents.

### 2. `SBR-CASE-ENV` and `SBR-CASE-SEC` are defined in one document and absent from the other, and this one blocks billing

The ID Standard defines neither. FinGov section 5 defines both, with worked examples,
and section 3.2 binds them to billing sub-codes 22 and 24.

Sub-code 22 cannot be billed without an identifier format for the case it bills. This
is not a naming preference. It stops revenue being recorded against those two case
types.

**What we need:** formats for both, or a decision that sub-codes 22 and 24 are not
billed yet.

### 3. `SBR-SRV-BUY` against `SBR-SRV-TYPE`

The ID Standard hard-codes `BUY`. FinGov section 5 writes `TYPE` with `BUY` as one
example value. The platform follows the standard, so **every service request it has
ever issued asserts it is a buyer request.**

If a service request can ever be a seller request or a due diligence request, the whole
issued estate is asserting something untrue about itself, and the fix is not a code
change alone.

**What we need:** `BUY` or `TYPE`, and if `TYPE`, what happens to the ones already
issued.

### 4. The standard contradicts itself on which register non-property identifiers draw from

Section 2.0 rule 6 implies the national register throughout. Sections 3.0 and 4.0 then
give their formats and examples using **Lagos property codes**. Section 7.0 gives its
examples, for those same identifier types, using **national codes**.

The two registers are disjoint, 32 Lagos codes and 38 national ones, so this is not a
preference between synonyms. It decides what a correct identifier looks like.

Until it is answered we are implementing the **property register mapping only** and
leaving every other location segment on its current behaviour behind a recorded gap,
rather than guessing.

**What we need:** which register each non-property identifier type draws from.

### 5. `SUR` means two different things inside the same document

Section 4.0 gives `SBR-SUR-...` for **Surveyor**. Section 5.0 gives `SUR` for
**Surulere**. A reader holding a `SUR` segment cannot tell from the identifier alone
which register it came from.

This is survivable if question 4 comes back with a hard rule about which register each
type draws from, and it is a live ambiguity if it does not, so please answer it
together with question 4. It should be cheap to rule on.

### A scoping decision that is yours, not engineering's

Some estate already carries a location code we now believe is wrong. **We are not
changing any of it**, and the story currently in front of us does not touch it either
way. We are raising it because the decision is yours and it is easier to make before
the volumes grow.

Two operations get conflated, and they are not the same thing:

- **Editing an identifier in place, or reusing a retired one, is forbidden.** Section
  2.0 rules 2 and 3 are unambiguous: identifiers are immutable once generated and are
  never reused. We are not proposing either.
- **Superseding a record is not forbidden.** The old identifier is retained and marked
  superseded, never reused. A new one is issued, linked to the old, with the reason
  recorded. Section 5.2's own rule list ends with "manual override requires documented
  approval and an audit event", so the standard contemplates this and governs it rather
  than banning it. Section 8.2's reversal and replacement rule is the same principle
  applied to financial records.

So the question is whether the mis-coded estate should be superseded, left as is with
the mapping corrected for new issuance only, or handled some third way.

**Before you decide, the reach is wider than either document discusses.** We would
rather you had this in front of you than after:

- **FinGov section 6.1 requires eight document filename patterns and every one of them
  embeds an identifier**: buyer information form, seller information form, final DD
  report, physical inspection report, escrow confirmation, seller settlement statement,
  commission invoice, VAT record. Superseding a record does not rename the documents
  already filed against it. Either the filed set keeps the old identifier and stops
  matching the register, or it is refiled.
- **Power of Attorney QR codes embed a verify URL pointing at a record.** Issued
  instruments are already out in the world and the runbook records that they cannot be
  recalled. A superseded identifier behind one of those URLs has to keep resolving.
- **Any external correspondence quoting an old identifier goes stale**, including
  anything already sent to a buyer, a seller, a lawyer or a bank.

We are not asking you to solve those three. We are asking you to weigh them, because a
supersession decision is cheap in the database and expensive everywhere else.

### Until this comes back

No identifier format is being changed except the property register mapping, and every
other location segment stays exactly as it behaves today, behind a recorded gap rather
than a guess.

## EXT-9

**To:** Finance
**Copy:** Digital Records, counsel (question 2 touches the Power of Attorney wording)
**Subject:** Commission basis, three questions. Four sources currently give three readings.

Finance,

We need the commission basis confirmed rather than inferred. Four sources describe it
and they do not agree.

| Source | What it says |
| --- | --- |
| The platform as built | 5% on the seller side only, no buyer commission at all |
| Power of Attorney clause 5 | the donee deducts 10% at source |
| Terms of service section 5(a) | a minimum of 5% from buyer and seller cumulatively |
| FinGov sub-codes 11 and 12 | separate codes for buyer and seller, which corroborates two-sided |

The likeliest reconciliation is that 5% plus 5% is the Power of Attorney's 10% at
source. That is plausible enough to be dangerous, which is why we are asking rather
than assuming.

1. **Is the commission one-sided or two-sided?** The platform charges the seller side
   only and has no buyer commission at all, despite sub-code 11 existing for it.
2. **If two-sided, is the buyer's 5% collected from the buyer, or withheld from the
   seller's proceeds alongside the seller's 5%?** The second is what "deduct from
   source 10%" would mean, and it is a materially different thing to tell a seller.
3. **Is "minimum of 5%" a floor with a configurable actual rate, or is it the rate?**
   If it is a floor, who sets the actual rate, and where is that decision recorded?

**What this blocks:** the commission and settlement story cannot be written until all
three are answered. We are not encoding a rate on the strength of the most plausible
reading.

## EXT-10

**To:** Finance **and** counsel, together
**Subject:** No signed instrument authorises withholding VAT from seller proceeds

This goes up separately from the commission question because it is not a rate question.
It is an authority question.

**No document the seller signs authorises the platform to withhold VAT from their
proceeds.** Power of Attorney clause 5 authorises the 10% brokerage deduction and stops
there. It says nothing about VAT.

FinGov section 4's formula and Appendix C's worked example both withhold VAT anyway. On
a NGN 50,000,000 sale:

| | Commission | VAT | Seller receives |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| FinGov Appendix C | 2,500,000 | 187,500 | **47,312,500** |
| The platform as built | 2,500,000 | none | **47,500,000** |

The difference is NGN 187,500 of a seller's money on a single transaction, and **the
platform's current behaviour is the one that matches the instrument the seller actually
signed.** The specification is the document out ahead of its authority, not the code.

**The question:** what authorises the withholding?

- If clause 5 was always intended to cover it, we need that confirmed in writing by
  counsel, because the clause does not say so.
- If it does not currently authorise it, the fix is a drafting change to clause 5 and a
  re-execution path for instruments already signed, not a configuration value.

Either way this has to be answered before any VAT rate is written into the platform.

## EXT-11

**To:** Legal
**Copy:** the client, Corne Labs
**Subject:** BLOCKING. Two data protection documents give different transfer rules and the stricter one is already being crossed.

Legal,

Marking this blocking because it is a live exposure rather than a future one.

The **internal data protection policy section 12** says personal data **shall not** be
transferred outside Nigeria unless one of three conditions is met: an adequacy
decision, NDPC-approved standard contractual clauses, or a valid statutory exception.

The **published privacy policy section 10** promises only that **reasonable steps** are
taken.

**The platform already runs on non-Nigerian infrastructure.** So the two documents are
not describing a choice we are about to make. They are describing something already
happening, and the stricter of the two is the one we cannot currently demonstrate
compliance with.

**Which rule governs?** A closed list of three conditions and a reasonable-steps
undertaking produce different infrastructure. We cannot choose a storage region until
this is answered, which is why the region decision and the document storage work are
both held.

**Two further conflicts go up with this one**, both in the published policy:

- It treats **browser settings as cookie consent**.
- It treats **continued browsing as consent**.

Neither is supported by the internal policy or by the NDPA.

**Engineering's position in the meantime** is to encode the stricter rule. If the answer
comes back strict we are already compliant; if it comes back permissive we have given
away nothing but flexibility. The tests will be the record of which document won, so
whichever way you rule, the reasoning stays visible to whoever reads this in a year.

**What we need:**

1. Which document governs cross-border transfer.
2. If the internal policy governs, which of the three conditions we are relying on for
   the infrastructure already in use, and what has to be in place before the next region
   decision.
3. A ruling on the two consent mechanisms.

## EXT-12

**To:** Management
**Subject:** Is the ID Standard currently being revised? Plus: whose company is this, and the domain we print onto instruments.

Two things, and the first is holding up work this week.

### 1. Is the ID Standard under revision right now?

There is a Word lock file sitting beside `SafeBuy Realties ID Standard.docx`, which
means the document is open on somebody's desk.

The next story encodes **32 Lagos codes and 38 national codes** straight out of that
document. If it is being revised, we wait for the revision rather than encode a version
about to be superseded and then do it twice.

**A yes or no is enough**, and it is the only thing standing between us and starting. If
it is being revised, we would also like to know roughly when, and whether the location
registers are among what is changing.

To be clear about why we are asking rather than checking: **a revision arrives as a new
file.** Comparing a checksum against the copy we hold can only tell us our copy has not
changed, never that a newer one does not exist. So this is a question only you can
answer.

The financial governance foundation already merged does not wait on this. Its tables
contain no location code at all.

### 2. The entity, the address and the canonical domain

Four documents give **three spellings of the company name** and **two registered
addresses**. Separately, the terms of service give the website as
`safebuyrealtiesltd.com`, while the platform bakes `safebuyrealties.com` into the QR
code on every Power of Attorney it generates.

**That second one is why further instrument generation is on hold.** The runbook already
records that issued instruments cannot be recalled. A Power of Attorney carrying a QR
code pointing at a domain the company does not control, or does not intend to use, is
not something we can fix after the fact. We would rather generate none than generate
more of those.

**What we need:**

1. Is the ID Standard being revised, yes or no.
2. The registered company name, exactly as it appears on the incorporation certificate.
3. The registered address, the same way.
4. The canonical domain, and confirmation of who controls it.

Items 2 to 4 also need to reach counsel, because the terms of service and the Power of
Attorney both carry the entity details and one of them needs amending regardless of
which answer comes back.
