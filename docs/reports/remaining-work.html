Remaining work: the eight things only somebody outside this team can do

Seventeen stories are left on the MVP and not one of them is a developer's to start. Every one is waiting on eight items that no amount of engineering can produce, because they are decisions, purchases, accounts, rates and documents that belong to somebody outside this team.

This is the fifth edition, and it exists because two days moved more than the four before them. On 5 August an approved financial governance specification arrived from outside this repository, answered ADR-0002 on its way past, and opened a ninth epic worth eleven to nineteen developer-days that had never been counted. On 6 August five escalations went out and all five came back the same day, which has not happened on this project before. Two of the five closed. Three did not. The backlog is larger than the fourth edition reported, the answer rate is the best it has ever been, and the number of stories a developer may start this morning is still zero.

What changed on 6 August: five answers in one day, and two of them closed something. What has not changed, and it is now the whole story. Before the figures: what every code on this page means

This project labels things tersely so that the board, the backlog and this report can all point at the same thing without ambiguity. That helps the people who read them daily and it slows down everybody else, so the whole vocabulary is written out here. Nothing below needs to be looked up anywhere else, and every code used in a figure is also spelled out where the figure uses it.

What each prefix means

ADR
Architecture Decision Record. A numbered, written-down decision, kept in docs/adr/. It exists so that a choice is made once, by a named person, with the reasoning attached, instead of being made five times by whoever is typing. An ADR that is still open is a piece of engineering that nobody is allowed to start.
EXT
External input. Something the team cannot produce with code no matter how many people are on it: an account with a supplier, a domain, a signed document, a booked appointment, a rate somebody with authority has to confirm. Numbered EXT-1 to EXT-12. Nine of the twelve are open.
E1 to E9
Epic. A theme of work. The nine of them are listed on the right.
-S1, -S2
Story within that epic. E6-S2 is the second story of epic 6. A story is the unit that becomes one pull request.
S, M, L
Size. Small is about a day, Medium two to four days, Large about a week. These are developer-days of scope, not calendar days, and the difference matters enough that figure 6 is about nothing else.
D1 to D5
Decision awaiting an answer. Each one maps to an ADR.
G1 to G6
Go-live gate. A condition that must be demonstrably true before launch. Listed on the right.
W1 to W5
Wave. A batch of stories a developer works through together. All five waves are closed. Wave 5 held one story and was opened by a document arriving rather than by a developer finding something, and no wave 6 can be scheduled until an item on the list below arrives.
QA-nnn
A numbered finding from the quality audit that produced this backlog.
DOCS-n, CH-n
Documentation and chore rows. Real pull requests that carry no engineering days, so they never move the totals in this report.

E2 Money
Money integrity. Sellers get paid into their own account, refunds genuinely reach the buyer's card, and finance can reconcile it.

Document trust. Uploaded documents survive a deploy, and a file claiming to be a PDF is checked rather than believed.

E7 Ops

Words borrowed from finance, law and email

escrow
Holding a buyer's money in a third account until both sides have done their part, instead of passing it straight to the seller. Whether this platform does that was ADR-0002, and it was a regulatory question as much as a technical one. It does. An approved specification obliges the platform to reconcile an escrow bank balance against the per-transaction sub-ledgers, and only the operator of an account can be put under that obligation.
sub-ledger
A running record of what is owed on one transaction, kept underneath a single real bank account that holds many transactions' money at once. Reconciliation is checking that the sub-ledgers add up to the bank balance. This is what E9-S2 builds.
VAT and WHT
Value Added Tax, charged on a service, and Withholding Tax, deducted at source and remitted on somebody's behalf. Which of the two applies to the platform's commission, and whether anything may be deducted from the seller's proceeds, is EXT-10. The answer that came back on 6 August says no deduction from the seller, and it is unsigned.
NDPA, GAID, NDPC
The Nigeria Data Protection Act 2023, the General Application and Implementation Directive 2025 made under it, and the Nigeria Data Protection Commission that enforces both. EXT-11 established that these govern rather than either company policy, which is what makes the region question in ADR-0004 answerable. 2026-08-07: it was answered. ADR-0006 put the bucket in a Nigerian region, so the statutory closed list never engages.
SCC
Standard contractual clauses. A written agreement with a hosting provider and its subprocessors that makes moving personal data outside Nigeria lawful. The platform already runs on non-Nigerian infrastructure and no such instrument exists in this repository. 2026-08-07: ADR-0006 moves it off that infrastructure, so no instrument is needed. This term stays in the glossary because it becomes the condition on ever putting production data outside Nigeria again, which is a different thing from a blocker.
PoA
Power of Attorney. The instrument by which a buyer authorises the platform to act on their behalf. Its wording was drafted by engineers in a build checklist, which is why EXT-4 asks counsel to look at it.
NDPR
Nigeria Data Protection Regulation. The 2019 regulation that E8-S1 was written against, now sitting under the NDPA 2023 and the GAID 2025 above it. It is why E8-S1 exists, and the platform stores identity documents and selfies today with no consent model, no retention policy and no way to erase anybody.
KYC
Know Your Customer. The identity check a regulated business performs before letting somebody move money. The records exist here; nothing reads them yet.
AML / CBN
Anti-Money Laundering obligations, and the Central Bank of Nigeria, the regulator whose rules decide what an escrow answer is allowed to be.
SPF, DKIM
Two DNS records that tell the rest of the internet that mail claiming to come from this domain really does. Without them, the platform's mail lands in spam or is rejected outright. They are part of EXT-3 and they take minutes to add.
pen test
Penetration test. A paid, independent attempt to break into the system, run by people who did not build it. EXT-6 is booking one.
reconciliation
Checking that what the platform thinks it moved and what the bank actually moved are the same number. E2-S5 is the screen that does it.
retention and erasure
How long personal data is kept, and how a person gets it deleted. Both are legal positions before they are code, which is why EXT-5 comes first.

Every decision and external input, by name

The eight on this page are marked in red. The rest are here so that a code seen elsewhere in the repository can be looked up in one place. Three of the red ones were answered on 6 August and are still red, because an answer that contradicts itself, or that needs a signature it does not carry, has not closed anything.

ADR-0001
Answered on 2 August. Is buying a property on the platform part of the MVP? Yes. It released E1's four stories and, behind them, E4-S2, and all five have merged. Recorded as Accepted in docs/adr/0001-mvp-scope-envelope.md.
ADR-0002
Answered on 5 August. Does the platform hold client funds, or pass them straight through? It holds them. Not by an opinion relayed second hand: the approved Financial Governance Specification obliges the platform to reconcile an escrow bank balance against the sum of the per-transaction sub-ledgers, and to carry that balance as a liability owed to the parties rather than as income. A platform that merely orchestrated payments between other people's accounts would have nothing to reconcile. E2-S1 is therefore a regulated flow with reconciliation duties rather than a bank-details form, which is its scope now rather than a risk it might have. What still holds those three stories is EXT-1.
ADR-0003
Payment mock-mode guard. Shipped. Production can no longer start in a mode that pretends payments succeeded. The record still reads Proposed, which is a paperwork gap rather than an open question.
ADR-0004
How are private documents served? Signed short-lived links from object storage, or streamed through the API with an authorization check on every read. It has to be decided before the bucket is bought, because the two answers need different bucket settings. Its region sub-decision was blocked until 6 August and is now open rather than blocked, which is not the same as decided: a Nigerian region needs no transfer instrument, and a non-Nigerian one may not carry production data until standard contractual clauses are executed and documented. Holds three stories and half of gate G3. 2026-08-07: the region was decided. ADR-0006 chose a Nigerian one, so no instrument is needed and the sub-decision is closed. The three stories are still parked and what they are parked behind is EXT-2. This ADR's own record still reads Proposed and its access half has still not been ratified, which is a paperwork gap rather than an open question.
ADR-0005
The quality ratchet. Enforced on every pull request. Coverage on changed lines cannot fall. The record still reads Proposed.
EXT-1
Production payment keys and a settlement account. It used to travel with ADR-0002 and no longer does, because that decision came back on 5 August. It is now the whole of what holds eleven developer-days of money work, and the account has a business verification behind it with its own lead time.
EXT-2
An S3-compatible bucket, its credentials and a region choice. Travels with ADR-0004. 2026-08-07: the region is no longer a choice. ADR-0006 fixed it as Nigerian, so what is left of this item is the bucket and the credentials, and it now holds its three stories on its own.
EXT-3
A transactional email domain, SPF, DKIM, DMARC and sending credentials. The single highest-value item on this page: it holds thirteen developer-days and it is the fastest of the eight to obtain.
EXT-4
Counsel-approved Power of Attorney text and terms of service. Holds one story worth a single day, and gate G4. Writing the ask down found that it is three documents rather than two, and that the third is the instrument this platform already generates and which counsel has never read.
EXT-5
A privacy notice and a retention policy under Nigerian data protection law. Holds the largest single blocked story on the board. Writing the ask down found that both documents already exist and are structurally complete, so what is missing is board adoption, a named data protection officer, a commencement date and a retention period for each of eight data categories.
EXT-6
A penetration test vendor and a booked window. Holds one story worth a single day, and gate G5. The only item on the list with nothing upstream of it, so it could be booked today.
EXT-7
Confirmation of D1 and D2. Half discharged on 2 August when D1 came back, and fully discharged on 5 August when the specification closed D2. Nothing waits on it.
EXT-8
Identifier rules, with Digital Records. Answered 6 August and not closed. Five conflicts between the ID Standard and the Financial Governance Specification, plus a published terms page promising users professional categories the platform cannot issue an identifier for. The answer contradicts itself in three places: Surveyor is coded SUR in one place and SVR in three others; an instruction says a UUID replaces the NNN sequence, printed beside four formats approved on the same pages that all end in NNN; and the fourteenth professional category is written without separators, with a segment the document never defines. None has been resolved toward either reading, because choosing one would put an invented rule into a register whose whole purpose is provenance. Holds E9-S2.
EXT-9
The commission rate, with Finance. Answered 6 August and not closed. The same returned document gives it as 5% per side in every response box and 10% per side in every collation row. On a fifty-million-naira sale that is the difference between NGN 2,500,000 and NGN 5,000,000 a side. The 5% reading reconciles with the instrument the seller actually signs and the 10% reading does not, and saying so is an observation about the instrument rather than this team picking a number. Holds E9-S3 and, behind it, E9-S4.
EXT-10
Tax treatment of the commission, with counsel. Answered 6 August and not closed. The ruling is that VAT is charged on the platform's service fee and nothing is withheld from the seller's proceeds, which is the answer the platform wanted and which overrules an acceptance criterion that assumed the opposite. It is headed a recommended counsel position for formal signature and carries no signature, so it is a draft of an answer rather than an answer. Travels with EXT-9.
EXT-11
Data residency. Answered twice on 6 August and closed. The NDPA 2023 and the GAID 2025 govern rather than either company policy, so the closed list of transfer conditions applies and the published policy's "reasonable steps" wording does not. The second reply named the mechanism: an NDPC-approved Cross-Border Data Transfer Instrument or standard contractual clauses with the hosting provider and the subprocessors. Closing it created three things that are not this question asked again: an instrument that does not exist, an amendment to the published privacy policy, and a policy effective date of platform go-live.
EXT-12
Company identity and the ID Standard's status. Answered 6 August and closed. Safebuyrealties International Ltd, RC 8483982, Ikeja, Lagos, and the canonical domain is www.safebuyrealtiesltd.com, which is not the domain this platform prints into every Power of Attorney QR code. Version 2 of the ID Standard is due 2026-08-13, and it supersedes on content rather than on data. The freeze on generating further signed instruments lifts when that default is corrected, not before.

Method

Where the numbers come from. The story list, its statuses, its dependencies and the per-epic effort ranges are read directly from docs/mvp-board.html and docs/MVP_OUTSTANDING_BACKLOG.md. The board is checked on every pull request by scripts/check-board.mjs, which fails if the totals on it stop agreeing with the rows underneath them, so the inputs to this page are gated rather than trusted.

How per-story days are derived. The backlog publishes effort as a range per epic, not per story. Each epic's upper bound is apportioned across its remaining stories in proportion to size, with Small counting one, Medium three and Large five, and any remainder given to the largest story in the epic. Every allocation lands inside its published size band. The result summed to sixty-one across the nineteen stories the third edition measured, which was exactly the board's published ceiling. Five of those stories merged and took seventeen days with them, leaving forty-four. The ninth epic then added nineteen across four stories, and one of those four merged the same day it was written and took four with it. Forty-four plus fifteen is fifty-nine across seventeen stories, which is again exactly the board's published ceiling. Nothing was re-estimated to get there; the same per-story figures are added up over a different list.

A two-day difference between the second and third editions, and it was not new work. The second edition's per-story figures summed to fifty-nine against a published ceiling of sixty-one, and it called the gap drift and left it. The gap was two developer-days lost to rounding, one in E7 and one in E8, when the remainder was spread evenly instead of given to the largest story. Fixing the arithmetic added two days to the total on paper and added no work at all. That fifty-nine and this edition's fifty-nine are different numbers over different story lists, and their agreeing is a coincidence.

Upper bounds throughout. Every figure on this page uses the ceiling of each range, not the floor. The floor across the same seventeen stories is thirty-seven developer-days, and it is on the board as the other end of the published range. The floor assumes every remaining story lands at the cheap end of its estimate, and no week in this repository has done that.

Lead times. The calendar figures for obtaining each external item are planning estimates, not quotes. They are deliberately optimistic: one day for a mail domain, one for a three-sentence reply on identifiers, two for a storage decision and a bucket, seven for a commission rate confirmed and a counsel position signed, twelve for a settlement account with a business verification behind it, fourteen for counsel, twenty-one for a privacy review and for a penetration test window. If any of them is wrong it is wrong in the direction of being too short, which makes the case in figure 4 stronger rather than weaker.

An answered item is not a shorter item. Three of the eight were asked on 6 August and answered the same day, and this page still counts their full lead time from this morning. That is deliberate. A reply that gives two different commission rates has not started the clock on the reply that gives one, and treating it as though it had would put a finish date on this page that nothing in the record supports.

How the work is staffed. One developer building with as many AI agents in parallel as the work allows, and a second developer reviewing and merging. That arrangement is why the calendar figures in figure 6 sit so far below the developer-day figures, and it is also why the stakeholder items dominate this report: when building gets fast, waiting is all that is left.
