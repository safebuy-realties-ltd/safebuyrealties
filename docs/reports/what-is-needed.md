## How to answer, and where

Two documents carry the response blocks: the closure schedule for eight of the items, and the decision request for three. The line marked WHERE THE ANSWER GOES on each item below says which one, by name. An answer written in the wrong document is still an answer, but it takes longer to record, because the record checks every answer against the question as it was originally asked.

This page also carries a writing space under every point. In the Word version those spaces are ruled lines you can type straight into, and the file can be filled in and sent back as it stands. In the web version they are typing boxes: you can type into them and print the page, but nothing you type is saved when the page is closed. If you are answering rather than reading, use the Word version.

### Five rules, and they are the same five printed on the schedule that went out

1. **Never leave a point blank.** A blank cannot be told apart from a point nobody read. If a point does not apply, write 'not applicable' and one line saying why. If the answer is not available today, write 'pending' and the date you expect it.
2. **Never write a secret value.** No key, password, secret or access token goes into this document, into an email body or into a chat message, in any point and in any circumstance. The points about credentials ask who received them, on what date and through which channel. That is the whole answer, and it is enough.
3. **Write dates in full.** 2026-08-14, not 'next week' and not 'Q3'. Other dates in the record are calculated from the dates you give.
4. **Name people, not departments.** 'Finance' cannot approve anything and 'Legal' cannot sign anything. A named person in a named role can. Every point that ends in an approval asks for a full name, a role and a date, for that reason and no other.
5. **If a point is wrong, say so in the point.** A correction is worth more to this project than a compliant answer to a question that should not have been asked.

### Signatures, and what counts as one

Where an item needs a signature, the exact wording to write is printed on the item, in quotation marks. Write that wording, then complete the four lines under it.

A handwritten signature is not required. A full name typed or written in the signature line, with the role and the date beside it, is accepted and carries the same weight. What the record needs is an attributable person, not an image of a pen stroke. This is the form the Chief Operating Officer used to accept ADR-0003 on 7 August 2026, and that acceptance is on the record and closed.

A signature given by somebody whose role is not stated cannot be recorded, because the record has no way to check that the person who signed was entitled to. The role line is not a formality.

### What the status words mean

| Word | What it means here |
|---|---|
| Open | The answer has not arrived, or part of it has arrived with nobody's name against it. An unattributed answer is treated as open. |
| Answered in part | Some points have come back and been recorded. The item stays open until the rest come back. Partial answers are welcome and they land immediately: one answered point can release the story behind it without waiting for the others. |
| Closed | Every point has an answer, and that answer carries a full name, a role and a date. The answer is then written into the repository, and the item stops appearing on pages like this one. |
| Blocked | The story cannot start. Starting it would mean writing down an answer that nobody has given, which is how a guess becomes a fact in a database. |
| Planned | The story can start on the day the story in front of it starts. Nobody outside the engineering team is holding it. |
| Holds | The stories that cannot start until this item closes. Read it as the cost of the item staying open. |
