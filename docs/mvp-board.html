The limit this week is not developer-days, it is review hours. A small PR costs 30 to 45 minutes to review properly, which caps one reviewer at 12 to 15 PRs across five days. The plan is sized to that, not to agent throughput.

Up next day 5, the last day

Day 3 closed in day 4. Seven PRs merged against two planned — E5-S2a (#102), the failing probe (#103), the public-asset gate on /uploads (#104), KYC and credential reads (#106), due diligence report reads (#108), plus env-file hygiene (#105) and a backlog reconcile (#107) — and at the end of all seven the day's actual deliverable was still open: E3-S1 says retire the public static route, and the route was still mounted. It came out in E3-S1d-3 (#112), two days and two sub-stories later. Seven merged PRs was not the same fact as a closed day, and this board said "done" until someone checked.

Two of those seven did less than their titles claimed. Both gaps are now closed or reduced. #105 widened backend/.gitignore only, so a root .env or .env.production stayed committable — and the root is exactly where env files are expected, since .env.example there tells you to make one and Vite reads the whole family. The one root name that was safe, .env.local, was safe by accident: it matched *.local, a Vite convention line rather than an env rule. CH-3 (#113) put the family pattern in the root file, where it covers every directory instead of one. #107's reconcile was sound, but it predated #108 and its suggested two-way split for E3-S1d has since been shown wrong — the backlog caught up in #112, and DOCS-6 (#116) has now closed that paragraph along with the rest of the sweep. Neither was a regression; both were the gap between what shipped and what the row said, which is the thing this board exists to make visible.

E3-S1 closed by deleting a route, not by adding one. What was left was not what the row said: the mount was held open by public listing imagery, the last thing on the frontend still building an /uploads/ URL. The obvious answer was a second, public delivery route beside the authorized one. E3-S1d-3 did the opposite — /api/v1/documents/file took optional authentication, so one route now decides per Document row and serves listing_hero and listing_gallery to a visitor with no session while refusing the title deed one row away. That absorbed the E3-S1b gate rather than leaving two gates on one prefix, which is how the next leak would have got in.

The coverage floor was measured, and the measurement was the finding. E7-S2 (#114) set thresholds just under today's numbers in both suites and made CI publish the reports. The backend was already measuring all 155 of its files: 41.7% of statements. The frontend was not measuring anything, and its default would have reported 48.06% — a number computed over the 20 files the tests happen to import, out of 185. Measured the way the backend measures, it is 4.91%. Both numbers are now written beside the thresholds that enforce them. The other half of the criterion, a strict bar on new and changed files, is diff coverage: no runner here can express it, so it is E7-S2b rather than a tick.

The board rule was updating three fields on a page that states everything six times. CH-5 (#115) is the answer to that. Rule 8 asked for the row's status, PR number and Day column, and a story's status also lives in the day card, the counter tiles, the header, the review queue and the prose above them — so an author who followed the rule exactly left five true-looking statements behind. Two were sitting on this page when the check went in: the go-live tile said 2 gates wait on external input where the list marks 3, and the review queue still led with #114, which had merged. Neither would ever have been caught by a rule that stops at the row. validate:board now checks every restated number against the rows, so the table in rule 8 is enforced rather than advised.

The backlog had gone stale in the direction that flatters nobody, and DOCS-6 (#116) is the reconcile. Seven rows still said planned about work that had merged, which is the harmless half. The harmful half was in the executive summary: it named three blockers, and by day 4 one of them was telling the reader that private documents are publicly readable — untrue since #112 deleted the mount. A document that overstates a closed risk is not a cautious document; it is one the next team stops believing on the first thing they check. Effort was re-derived rather than re-guessed: each merged story subtracted at its published size, so the new Remaining column sits beside the original estimate and means the same kind of thing. 72–100 becomes 67–97, and the tile and the bars below now read from it.

A week of merging bought five days off the floor and three off the ceiling, and Ops got longer. Six milestone stories landed and five of them were S; the one M turned into six sub-stories. Meanwhile E7 went from 10–14 to 10–16, because measuring the coverage floor found the half of its own criterion a floor cannot express and that is now E7-S2b. Both of those are the shape of an audit week rather than a mis-estimate. The check gained a tooth to match: validate:board now requires the Full backlog tile's ceiling to equal the sum of the effort bars, which is CH-shaped work inside a DOCS story and is flagged as such in the PR — it earned its place by catching the drift already there, bars summing to 103 under a tile claiming 100.

Day 5 holds all 4 remaining PRs, and one reviewer can absorb 4 to 6. The week was sized at 14 and 21 have merged. Days 1 to 4 are closed and every security row is in; what is left is a packaging day that is over the ceiling. Order of value if something has to go: E7-S5 and DOCS-4 are what the next team opens on their first morning; E7-S3 is a timeboxed spike whose deliverable can be a written finding; E7-S4 is the one to cut first, and its dependency needs a decision either way — the row lists E3-S2, durable object storage, which is not scheduled this week. If the seed only has to resolve locally the dependency is soft and the story is an M; if it has to resolve on Vercel it cannot land at all.

Carry these two rules into day 5. Pass --base main on every gh pr create, and run gh pr list --json number,title,baseRefName --jq '.[] | select(.baseRefName != "main")' before reviewing each day — silence means every open PR targets main. That one-second check would have caught the #100 failure immediately. And every story PR now brings the whole of this page up to date in the same diff — the row, its day card, the tiles, the header, the queue and any count the prose quotes — with npm run validate:board locally and enforced in CI, because every time this board went stale it was found by a person rather than by a check.

Agent working agreement hand this to every agent alongside the story

  1. One story, one PR, single purpose. Never combine two stories to save a round trip
  2. Copy the acceptance criteria verbatim into the PR description and tick them individually. Untickable criteria mean the story was not understood, which is a signal worth having early
  3. No story is done without pasted output from npm run validate:tsc, npm test, and cd backend && npm test
  4. Cite a file and line for every behavioural claim, matching the backlog's own standard
  5. If a story turns out bigger than its size, stop and say so rather than expanding scope
  6. Never run prisma migrate reset. The database is shared across local, preview and production
  7. If the change is user-facing and there is still no feature-flag system, say so rather than inventing one

The three launch blockers none are fixable this week, all are now documented

Critical

The on-platform journey does not finish

DueDiligenceService has one method, create(). Orders raised against a platform listing have no queue, no assignment, no report, no completion, so no transaction reaches DD_COMPLETE, the buyer is never offered the property purchase, escrow is never funded and the seller is never paid.

Critical

The money cannot safely move

Payouts resolve the destination from two environment variables defaulting to Paystack's test account, so every seller is paid to the same place. Refunds change a status and never call the gateway. The webhook has no replay guard. E2-S4 on day 2 removes the worst of it by refusing to start in production without a key.

Critical

Private documents are publicly readable

Uploads are served by an unauthenticated static route, so any storage key fetches a title deed, a government ID or a KYC selfie with no session. Day 3 attempts the fix and ships the failing probe test either way, so the hole is executable knowledge, not a paragraph.
